## PRODUCTION NOTES

- **Meter:** anapestic tetrameter — da-da-DUM da-da-DUM da-da-DUM da-da-DUM. Tap it out
  before reading. Where a line drops its opening unstressed syllable, that's deliberate.
- **The twins never exceed three words.** This is a hard rule, not a stylistic tic. It is
  how real two-year-olds talk, it keeps the two of them distinguishable in audio, and it
  means a two-year-old listener can repeat every line their character has.
- **SHLOOP** is the signature sound. Identical every single time, on the same beat every
  single time — a sound being taken. Never vary it. Repetition is the whole point.
- **PBBBBBT** is the escape hatch. It is the loudest, silliest, most important sound in
  the book.
- **The loudness curve is the emotional curve.** Spread 1 is busy and warm. It thins as
  the Hushabaloo wins. Spread 7 is the quietest thing in the book. Spread 8 is the
  loudest — but it *swells* into, it never slams. See the startle spec in
  `research/03-audio-sfx-and-elevenlabs.md`.
- **Page turns are cliffhangers.** Several spreads end mid-thought on purpose.

## COVER

**[NARRATOR]** *(warm, slow, letting it land)*

The Hushabaloo.

*(pause)*

Behind the third door on the left of the hall.

## SPREAD 1 — "A house that was full of it"

**[NARRATOR]**

In a house at the end of a hall that was long,
where the stairs had a squeak and the kettle a song,
there was Etta, who planned. There was Mo, who could hear.
There was Kip, who climbed everything, year after year.

*(building, delighted — this is an inventory of everything we are about to lose)*

And the house was so full of so much to be heard —
of a clank and a clink and a creak and a bird,
of the hum of the fridge, of the drip of the tap,
of the whumpf of the dog as he flopped for a nap.

*(slowing — this one matters)*

And the best of them all, and remember this trick,
was the door down the hall. And the door went —

> **[SFX: CLICK]** *— clean, small, wooden. This exact sound returns twice more.*

**[NARRATOR]**

— click.

---

## SPREAD 2 — "Tuesday"

**[NARRATOR]**

But on Tuesday the kettle did not sing its song.
And the stairs did not squeak. And the tap-drip was gone.

**[ETTA]** *(counting on fingers, not yet worried — interested)*

That's three. That's three sounds. And they've all gone away.

**[NARRATOR]**

Now the hall had three doors, and they knew one and two.
But the third door — the third door they'd never been through.
And they stood there. And Mo put his ear to the wood.

**[MO]** *(quiet, certain)*

Listen.

*(beat)*

Somefing in there.

**[NARRATOR]** *(dry)*

And there was.

*(the first one — let it be a surprise)*

For a sound they had heard every day of their lives —
the small squeak of the stairs — came unstuck, and it dived
through the crack of that door with a —

> **[SFX: SHLOOP]** *— the signature. A sound being swallowed. Wet, round, oddly satisfying.*

**[NARRATOR]**

— and was gone.
And the stairs were as silent as snow, from then on.

---

## SPREAD 3 — "A room that was not in the house"

**[NARRATOR]**

So they opened the door, and the door made no sound,
and they stepped through the door, and they looked all around.
And behind the third door on the left of the hall
was a room that was not in the house. Not at all.

*(slower — this is the awe beat)*

It was tall as a church and as wide as a town,
and it went up so far that no ceiling came down.
And on every last shelf, in a row, in a row,
were the jars. And each jar had a label. And so —

**[ETTA]** *(reading them off, thrilled — she loves a label)*

That one's RAIN. That one's DOG. That one's stairs-when-you-creep.
That one's somebody snoring. That's somebody's sheep!

**[NARRATOR]** *(quieter — the reveal, gently)*

Then a shelf became shoulders. A jar became eye.
And the thing they'd been sure was a wall said —

**[HUSHABALOO]** *(soft, delighted, faintly wheezy — he has not had visitors in a century)*

Oh. Hello. Oh my.

---

## SPREAD 4 — "The keeper"

**[HUSHABALOO]** *(warm, explaining something obvious and lovely)*

I am only the keeper. I keep them. That's all.
Every sound ever let loose in somebody's hall,
every squeak, every drip, every creak, every knock —
I have kept them all safe. Every one. Every clock.

**[NARRATOR]**

And he reached out a hand that was mostly a sleeve,
and he took Kip's small laugh. And he did not ask leave.

> **[SFX: SHLOOP]**

**[NARRATOR]**

And he took Mo's low hum.

> **[SFX: SHLOOP]**

**[NARRATOR]**

And then, gentle and quick,
he took Etta's own word, in the middle. Like this.

> **[SFX: SHLOOP]**

**[NARRATOR]** *(hushed — the trap closes and nobody notices)*

And behind them, so soft that not one of them heard,
he reached out once again. And he took — from the door —
the small click that it made.

> **[SFX: SHLOOP]** *— quieter than the others. This is the one that matters.*

**[NARRATOR]**

And the door was no more.

*(flat, matter-of-fact, three beats)*

For a door with no click isn't really a door.
It's a wall. It's a wall. It's a wall. Nothing more.

---

## SPREAD 5 — "Please"

**[NARRATOR]**

Now Etta was five, which is old. And she knew
that when somebody's taken a thing that's not theirs,
you say *give it back, please.* So she said it. She did.

**[ETTA]** *(polite, firm, absolutely certain this will work)*

Give them back. Give them back, please. They're ours.

**[HUSHABALOO]** *(genuinely baffled — he is not refusing, he does not understand the question)*

But they're safe. Don't you see? They are safe here with me.
Nothing's lost. Nothing ever is lost here. Not one.
I have rain from a Tuesday two hundred years gone.
It's still raining. In there. It has never been done.

**[ETTA]** *(pressing, exasperated)*

But a sound isn't safe when it's kept in a —

> **[SFX: SHLOOP]** *— comic timing. It takes the word she needed, mid-sentence.*

**[NARRATOR]** *(dry, sympathetic)*

And the word that she needed went off in a jar.
And she opened her mouth.

*(beat)*

And there wasn't a sound.

---

## SPREAD 6 — "Kip climbs, because Kip climbs"

**[NARRATOR]**

Then Kip sized the shelf up. Now, Kip is quite small.
But of all of the things about Kip, above all —

**[KIP]** *(flat, obvious, already going)*

I can get it.

**[NARRATOR]**

— Kip climbs. And Kip climbed. And Kip went
up a shelf, and another, and up, and he leant

**[KIP]** *(breathless, delighted, three shelves up)*

Watch me! Watch me!

**[NARRATOR]**

— and he reached for a jar,
and he stretched, and he wobbled, and grabbed, and —

**[KIP]** *(pure joy, no fear whatsoever)*

Uh oh.

> **[SFX: POP]** *— a jar breaking open. Bright, glassy, NOT a smash. Then rain rushing out.*

**[NARRATOR]** *(fast, exhilarated)*

And the jar came down hard, and the jar came apart,
and out came the rain! All the rain! From the start!
And it went up, and out, and it went through the wall,
to the window it came from, right down the long hall.

**[ETTA]** *(the discovery — this is information, not victory)*

It went HOME. Did you see it? It broke and went home!

---

## SPREAD 7 — "The quiet part"

*(This spread is the quietest thing in the book. Let it be quiet.)*

**[NARRATOR]**

But the Hushabaloo sighed. And he lifted the shelf.
And he put it up high, where a person can't climb.
And he said it so softly, and mostly himself —

**[HUSHABALOO]** *(not angry — heartbroken. This must not sound like a threat.)*

Please don't break them. Please don't.
I have had them a very long time.

**[NARRATOR]** *(slow, and slower)*

And it got very dark. And it got very still.
And the three of them sat. And they sat. And they sat.
And there wasn't a squeak. And there wasn't a drip.
And there wasn't a sound in the world.

*(beat)*

Just like that.

**[KIP]** *(small — the first time Kip has been small)*

I want home.

**[MO]** *(smaller)*

Me too.

**[NARRATOR]**

And Etta, who's five, and who plans, and who's brave,
had no plan. And she said it out loud, very small —

**[ETTA]** *(honest. This is the bottom.)*

I'm scared too.

**[NARRATOR]** *(the turn — same spread, no waiting)*

But then Mo, who hears things that the rest of us miss,
put his hand on a jar. And he listened. Like this.

**[MO]** *(quiet, but this is the hinge of the whole book)*

Etta.

*(beat)*

This one empty.

**[ETTA]** *(sitting bolt upright)*

...Empty? But he's got a jar for *everything.*

**[NARRATOR]**

And Etta's eyes went round and wide.

**[ETTA]** *(fast, building, delighted — the idea arriving in real time)*

He's got jars for the rain and the drip and the door —
for the sounds that things MAKE. But he hasn't got more!
He's got nothing at all for a sound that's not FOR
anything! Nothing! He's not heard one before!

*(turning to her brothers. Absolutely serious.)*

Kip. Mo. On three.

*(beat)*

Do the rudest thing you know.

---

## SPREAD 8 — "The rudest thing they know"

**[NARRATOR]** *(fast, all three at once)*

So Kip climbed. Because Kip climbs. And Mo found the jar —
because Mo always finds them. That's just what Mo does.
And Etta said —

**[ETTA]** *(the countdown — big)*

THREE. TWO. ONE —

**[NARRATOR]**

— and the twins, who were two,
did the one thing that two-year-old people do best.

> **[WAIT — 3s]** *Player pauses here for the listeners. Then Kip and Mo do it anyway.*

**[KIP + MO]** *(with everything they have)*

PBBBBBBBBT!

> **[SFX: RASPBERRY]** *— layered under the voices. Wet, absurd, enormous.*

**[NARRATOR]** *(building — this is the thesis, and it must land)*

And it wasn't a squeak. And it wasn't a drip.
And it wasn't the rain, and it wasn't a door.
It was nothing! It meant nothing! It came from a lip
and it went nowhere useful — a wonderful roar
of a silly, wet, pointless, ridiculous noise
that had no jar at all.

**[NARRATOR]** *(the Hushabaloo searching — slower, almost tender)*

And he looked for a jar.
And he looked. And he looked. And he looked down the hall
of ten thousand jars —

*(beat)*

and there wasn't one. Not one.
Not a label. Not one. Not at all. Not a jar.

**[NARRATOR]** *(release — the swell, never a slam)*

And a thing with no jar is a thing you can't keep.

> **[SFX: POP CASCADE]** *— one pop, then two, then a hundred. Rises over ~4 seconds.*

**[NARRATOR]** *(joyful, headlong)*

And the empty jar popped. And the next. And the next.
And the rain and the dog and the kettle and drip
and the whumpf and the hum and the squeak of the stair
went UP, and went OUT, and went HOME through the air.

---

## SPREAD 9 — "Click"

**[NARRATOR]** *(from the roar, down to almost nothing)*

And in all of that noise — in that whole rushing flood —
there was one little sound that was smaller than most.

**[MO]** *(perfectly calm. Of course he heard it.)*

Listen.

*(beat)*

There.

**[NARRATOR]**

And Mo heard it. Of course. Because that's what Mo does.
And he pointed. And there, in the dark, was a —

> **[SFX: CLICK]** *— the same click from Spread 1. Identical file. This is the payoff.*

**[NARRATOR]**

— click.
And a door with a click is a door once again.

*(the turn — they don't just leave)*

But they stood in the doorway. And turned. And they saw
that the Hushabaloo hadn't got anything more.
He had shelves. He had jars. He had none of them full.
And he sat down. And he was extremely small.

**[ETTA]**

Wait.

**[NARRATOR]**

And she walked all the way back, and stood by his knee.

**[ETTA]** *(gentle — this is the kindest line in the book)*

You can keep it. That one. That one's from me.

**[NARRATOR]**

And she blew him a raspberry, right through the air.

> **[SFX: RASPBERRY]** *— small, sweet, close-mic'd. A gift, not a weapon.*

**[NARRATOR]**

And he caught it. And held it. And kept it. Right there.
And he put it in nothing. No jar and no lid.
Just held it — which is, I suppose, what you do
with a sound that was *given* and not taken.

*(beat)*

He did.
And he smiled. If a Hushabaloo can. And he knew.

**[NARRATOR]** *(home — warm, and slowing all the way down)*

So they went down the hall to the house that was theirs,
where the kettle had a song, and a squeak had the stairs,
where the pipes gave a knock, and the tap gave a drip,
and the dog gave a whumpf —

> **[SFX: CLICK]** *— third and last. Home.*

**[NARRATOR]**

— and the door gave a click.

## FINAL PAGE

**[NARRATOR]** *(quiet, direct, to the listener)*

For everybody small who was ever once told

*(beat)*

here's a story where noise is the thing that saves all.

*(warm, and then waiting)*

Go on.

*(beat)*

Make the sound. You know the one.

> **[WAIT — 4s]** *The book waits. Whatever happens, happens.*

> **[SFX: RASPBERRY]** *Last sound in the book.*
